# Black holes, white holes, and universes inside universes

The physics this repo's other pages lean on. Plain claims, labelled: **[established]** textbook physics with
observational support, **[math only]** a valid solution nobody expects to see, **[speculative]** published
research that is unproven. Next: HORIZONS.md (what this has to do with trust), OZ.md (the same thing as a story).

## 1. Black holes [established]

General relativity: mass and energy curve spacetime (Einstein field equations, G_mn = 8πG/c⁴ · T_mn). Black
holes are solutions of those equations.

- **Schwarzschild (1916)**, no spin and no charge. The horizon sits at r_s = 2GM/c². That's about 3 km for the
  Sun and about 9 mm for Earth.
- **Kerr (1963)**, spinning, which is what real black holes are. Outside the horizon is an ergosphere, where
  spacetime itself is dragged around.
- **No-hair theorem.** From outside, a black hole is fully described by **mass, spin and charge**. Everything
  else about what fell in is invisible.
- **Orbits.** The photon sphere is at 1.5 r_s, the innermost stable orbit at 3 r_s (no spin). The photon sphere
  sets the size of the shadow in the Event Horizon Telescope images (M87* 2019, Sgr A* 2022).
- **Time dilation.** A hovering clock ticks slower by √(1 − r_s/r), and the factor goes to zero at the horizon.
- **Hawking radiation (1974)** [established in theory, not yet observed]: T = ħc³ / (8πGMk_B). Bigger black
  holes are colder.
- **Entropy** S = k_B c³ A / (4Għ). It scales with the horizon's **area**, not its volume. This leads to the
  Bekenstein bound (the most information a region can hold) and to the information paradox (is information
  that falls in destroyed, or only scrambled?), which is still open.
- **Gravitational waves** from merging black holes (LIGO, 2015 onward) match numerical relativity.

## 2. White holes [math only]

The full Schwarzschild solution (Kruskal–Szekeres coordinates) has four regions: our universe, the black hole
interior, a mirror universe, and a **white hole**, the black hole run backwards in time. Nothing can enter it,
and everything leaves it. The two universes connect through an Einstein–Rosen bridge, which pinches off too
fast for even light to cross.

Why nobody expects to see one: a collapsing star produces only the black hole half; white holes are unstable
(infalling matter piles up at the horizon and collapses it, Eardley 1974); and they run against the second law
of thermodynamics.

## 3. The cosmic horizon [established]

There is no centre of the universe. Space expanded everywhere at once, so every observer sees the same
picture: galaxies receding in every direction and a horizon at the same distance. The horizon is centred on
whoever is looking.

A black hole horizon keeps you out. A cosmic horizon keeps you in: you can't see past it, because the space
beyond is receding faster than light can close the gap. They share mathematics. Both have a temperature and
an entropy (Gibbons–Hawking, 1977).

A coincidence that keeps the next section alive: the Schwarzschild radius of the observable universe's mass is
about the Hubble radius. Mostly this follows from the universe being near critical density, and it is not
evidence by itself.

## 4. Universes inside black holes [speculative]

- **Pathria (1972):** our universe may be the inside of a black hole in a parent universe.
- **Poplawski (2010):** with torsion (Einstein–Cartan gravity), collapsing matter bounces instead of reaching a
  singularity, and the bounce expands into a new universe. From inside, that bounce looks like a Big Bang:
  the white hole side of someone else's black hole.
- **Smolin, cosmological natural selection (1992; *The Life of the Cosmos*, 1997):** every black hole spawns a
  child universe with slightly mutated constants, so universes good at making black holes come to dominate.
  It is testable in principle: it predicts no neutron star much heavier than about 2 solar masses. The
  heaviest known are about 2.0–2.3, so the test is close but not decided.
- **Eternal inflation (Linde, Vilenkin, Guth):** a different kind of bubbles. Universes sit side by side in an
  endlessly inflating space rather than nested inside each other. Searches of the cosmic microwave background
  for bubble-collision marks have found nothing convincing.
- **Loop quantum gravity bounces (Rovelli and others):** black holes may turn into white holes at the end of
  their lives, slowed by time dilation to billions of years from our point of view.

Problems: nobody knows what happens inside a black hole; the inheritance of constants has no mechanism; and
looking into a black hole, or out of our own universe, may be impossible in principle.

## 5. The shape that carries over

Strip out the physics and one shape is left:

> **From inside, you can't see your parent. From outside, you get a few numbers and nothing else. The only way
> out is through.**

HORIZONS.md takes that shape into software and trust.
